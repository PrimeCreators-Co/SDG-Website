SDG 3: Good Health & Well-being

Ensure healthy lives and promote well-being for all at all ages.

A Global Commitment to Health

SDG 3 is not just about treating diseases; it's about creating a world where everyone can live a healthy life. It's a comprehensive goal that addresses a wide range of health priorities, from maternal and child health to infectious diseases and mental health. By focusing on SDG 3, we are investing in a healthier, more prosperous, and sustainable future for all.

"Health is a state of complete physical, mental and social well-being and not merely the absence of disease or infirmity."

- World Health Organization

Key Areas of Focus

Maternal & Child Health

Reducing maternal and child mortality rates is a top priority. This includes ensuring access to skilled birth attendants, prenatal and postnatal care, and essential medicines.

Infectious Diseases

The fight against infectious diseases like AIDS, tuberculosis, and malaria is far from over. We need to strengthen health systems and ensure access to prevention, treatment, and care.

Non-Communicable Diseases

The burden of non-communicable diseases like heart disease, cancer, and diabetes is growing. We need to promote healthy lifestyles and ensure access to affordable treatment.

Mental Health

Mental health is an integral part of overall health. We need to break the stigma surrounding mental illness and ensure access to quality mental health care.

Success Stories

Reducing Child Mortality in Rwanda

Rwanda has made remarkable progress in reducing child mortality. Through a combination of community-based health insurance, performance-based financing for health facilities, and a strong focus on maternal and child health, the country has achieved a 70% reduction in child mortality since 2000.

Challenges Ahead

Funding Gaps

There is a significant funding gap for achieving SDG 3. More domestic and international investment is needed to strengthen health systems and ensure access to essential health services.

Health Worker Shortages

Many countries face a shortage of trained health workers, particularly in rural and remote areas. We need to invest in training and retaining health workers to ensure that everyone has access to quality care.

Global Health Crises

The COVID-19 pandemic has highlighted the need for stronger global health security. We need to be better prepared to prevent, detect, and respond to future health crises.
